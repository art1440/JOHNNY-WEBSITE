# Finding a world: examples

How to read these: audience → things they know by heart → the one picked → what it gives the page.
Each world is one honest option, not the answer. The point is the move from "category" to "their life".

## Hispanic families buying life insurance (the source project)
Candidates: painted houses of Latin American towns, talavera tile, family photo wall, embroidered tortilla
napkins (servilleta bordada), hand-painted shop signs (rótulos), Barragán's colored walls and light.
Picked **Casa de colores**: saturated lime-washed walls, zócalo bands, arched windows and doors, bougainvillea,
talavera trim. Gives: the quiz in an arched window, a tile license plaque, postcard testimonials, a door CTA.
Runner-up was the embroidered napkin (cross-stitch flowers, running stitches that "sew" themselves on scroll).

## Neighborhood bakery
Candidates: pastry case glass and paper doilies, flour-dusted wooden counter, string-tied pastry boxes, chalkboard
menus, the morning ticket number dispenser. Pick the **pastry box**: kraft and printed-stripe box sides as sections,
string that unties to open the menu, an order ticket ("take a number") as the call to action.

## Independent auto repair shop
Candidates: service invoices with carbon copies, pegboard tool walls, painted bay numbers, tire-tread marks,
the oil-change window sticker. Pick the **window sticker + work order**: the booking form is a work order with
stamped fields; "next service" stickers become the section headers.

## Kids' swim school
Candidates: lane ropes, pool tile and depth markers, chlorine-blue water caustics, kickboards, ribbon badges.
Pick **pool tile and depth markers**: sections labeled like depth markers, lane-rope dividers, light caustics
drifting slowly as the ambient motion, ribbons for each level.

## Accountant for small businesses
Candidates: ledger paper, receipt rolls, rubber "PAID" stamps, file folders with tabs, the shoebox of receipts.
Pick the **ledger and receipt roll**: green-ruled ledger grid, numbers in tabular figures, pricing on a printed
receipt that unrolls, a "PAID" stamp as the confirmation animation.

## Quick prompts to generate candidates
- What does this audience's kitchen, street, church, workplace or phone screen look like?
- What paperwork, packaging or signage do they handle every week?
- Which local design tradition (sign painting, tiles, textiles, posters, transit maps) do they grow up with?
- What object already means "this person keeps their promises" in their world (a stamp, a key, a door, a handshake
  photo on the wall)? That object usually makes the best trust device.
